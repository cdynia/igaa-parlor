It's Time to Travel · ETA

Draw a time card, move your pawn onto the clock, and spin to count down your ETA. Write the time the card and spinner make, in digital and analog, to keep the card. Reach your destination with 4 cards marked and ETA 0.

How to play

So, where are you going?

Start

Everyone starts in the START / REST AREA with the same trip ETA in minutes, written on their Player's ETA Card.

Your turn

  • Draw a time card and move your pawn to that time on the clock.
  • Spin for minutes to take off your ETA:
1 = 5 min2 = 10 min3 = 15 min0 = 30 min
  • Write the time. For 9–11 AM cards the minutes count TO the next hour. A 9 AM card and a spin of 1 is 9:55, "5 min to 10". Also write it as PAST: "55 min past 9". For 1–3 PM cards the minutes count PAST the hour. A 1 PM card and a spin of 1 is 1:05, "5 min past 1".
  • Write it correctly and you keep the card: mark C and take the minutes off your ETA.

Player's ETA Card codes

CCard. Your pawn goes on the matching hour; subtract the spin. RRest Area. Another player landed on your hour. Go to the Rest Area and spin to add minutes. The player who landed then takes their normal turn. If you've already been sent there 3 times, anyone landing on your hour goes to the Rest Area instead. LLunch. You drew a 12 NOON card: write "12" and spin to add lunch minutes. Draw another 12 NOON later and you just lose a turn, with no mark. SSpeeding. A 0 on this turn's travel spin after a 0 last turn: you're caught speeding and add 30 minutes. TTraffic. The same time card as your last turn: spin to add minutes. The card still counts toward your 4. If you were sent to the Rest Area since your last turn, this rule is skipped.

Winning

When the time cards run out, reshuffle them. The first player with 4 cards marked (C or T) and ETA = 0 reaches their destination and wins.

Digital edition notes

  • The ETA never goes below 0. Spins after that still earn marked cards.
  • "Same card" means the same hour (any color).
  • A speeding turn and a missed time answer don't mark the card.
  • The time challenge gives two tries. Computer players always write the time correctly.
